Finance analytics case study · public research extract · not a client engagement

Can this retail extract support a revenue number?

Question: for the UCI Online Retail gift wholesaler, what merchandise revenue is left after cancellations, non-product lines, bad prices, and a partial final day — and does that daily mart tie back to the invoice lines to the penny?

These figures are aggregates from the public file, not a forecast and not a business result. Nobody adopted this pipeline. It does not estimate ROI. The retailer is the one in the UCI citation, not a client of this portfolio.

Duplicate sensitivity

The forecast-matching total keeps exact duplicate extras, because that is what the published daily series does. Removing them would change the total. That difference is a decision, not a discovered fact about the shop floor.

What was left out, and what was kept without a customer

  • Partial final day rows: 1,632. The file's last timestamp is before 16:00, so that calendar day is not a full trading day.
  • Non-product stock codes (postage, fees, manual codes, gift vouchers, letter-prefixed goods): 2,983 rows.
  • Invoice numbers starting with A, including the bad-debt adjustments: 3 rows, signed amount -£11,062.06. They are not in the merchandise total.
  • Other returns inside the merchandise total: 0 lines, £0.00. Negative quantities that are not cancellation invoices and did not survive the rules: 1,324 with a non-positive price, 12 with a non-product code.
  • Unparseable date, quantity, or price: 4. Prices with a currency symbol, a comma, or more than two decimal places are not rounded into the ledger. Bad parsed prices (zero or negative): 2,485.
  • Kept lines with no customer id: 130,547 rows, £1,491,723.13. They are in revenue. They are not in the cohort.

Customers who can be followed

4,361 customer ids have at least one kept merchandise line. 1,122 of them (25.7%) also have kept merchandise in the following calendar month. That is a one-month repeat count on this extract, not a retention rate for a subscription and not a lifetime value.

How the number is rebuilt

  1. raw stores every source field as text, plus a row in load_run for each refresh.
  2. staging parses quantity and price to integers, flags cancellations, returns, missing customers, and exact duplicates, and assigns one disposition.
  3. mart is the daily calendar (gaps filled with zero), the customer cohort, the country ranking, and the checks.

SQLite is the whole warehouse. The extract is one file of about half a million rows. A hosted warehouse would not change the SQL and would add an account.

SELECT
  (SELECT SUM(line_amount_pence) FROM staging.stg_invoice_line
    WHERE disposition IN ('kept_merchandise', 'kept_duplicate_extra'))
  -
  (SELECT SUM(net_merchandise_pence) FROM mart.daily_revenue)
  AS difference_pence;

Offline fixture and tests: python -m finance.run from the repository root. Rebuild this page from the workbook: python -m finance.run --uci path/to/Online\ Retail.xlsx. The workbook is not in git. Limits

  • No payment date, tax, or currency rate. There is nothing here to age as accounts receivable, and nothing to convert from another currency.
  • Exact duplicates may be real repeated lines or a double-loaded extract. Both treatments are reported. Neither is labeled as cash collected.
  • Customer id is the public research identifier. Published outputs are aggregates. The local SQLite files that still contain those ids are gitignored.
  • The 19-row fixture under finance/fixtures plants a return, a currency symbol, and a comma decimal so the parser is tested. Those planted rows are not in the totals on this page.
